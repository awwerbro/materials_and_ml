**Summary:** 
Discussion focused on applicability and limitations of self-driving labs. Current systems are mature for optimization tasks (chemistry, drug discovery) but primarily handle scenarios with defined experimental patterns. Key concerns include: hyperparameter sensitivity (results must be independent of algorithmic choices), LLM limitations for generating novel hypotheses (constrained by training data), and the gap between optimization and true discovery.

Practical challenges for materials science were highlighted: complex synthesis and characterization workflows resist full automation, and two-week deposition processes don't suit algorithm-driven iteration. While automation aids routine tasks, creative scientific discovery still requires human intervention.

Broader implications include resource imbalances (self-driving labs favor well-funded institutions) and potential disruption to research practices. Smaller disruptive teams may be at a disadvantage. Suggested strategy: tailor experiments to be automation-friendly rather than retrofitting complex workflows to existing systems.
