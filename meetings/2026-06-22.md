# June 22, 2026 Meeting

**Date:** 2026-06-22  
**Presenter:** Eleni Poupaki, Eindhoven University of Technology  
**Topic:** Using Large Language Models for Chemical Data Extraction from Scientific Literature  
**Paper/Resources:** 
- [From Text to Insight: Large Language Models for Chemical Data Extraction](https://doi.org/10.1039/D4CS00913D) (primary paper discussed)
- [Edison Scientific Literature Agent](https://www.edison.science/) - AI-powered research assistant with academic credits
- [Undermind](https://www.undermind.ai/) - AI literature search tool
- [TabPFN](https://github.com/PriorLabs/TabPFN) - Tabular data prior-fitted network
- [Agent reasoning investigation preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18805)
- [Practical implementation of this by Sameer Sadruddin, Eleni Poupaki, and others](https://doi.org/10.1116/6.0005366)


**Summary:** 
Eleni presented a comprehensive workflow for extracting structured data from scientific papers using Large Language Models (LLMs), addressing the gap between the vast amount of research published and the limited availability of machine-readable datasets. The three-stage workflow covers: (1) preprocessing—converting PDFs to markdown format, removing unnecessary sections, and chunking text appropriately; (2) LLM interaction—using zero-shot (quick but basic), few-shot (medium quality with examples), or chain-of-thought (high quality with reasoning) prompting strategies; and (3) post-processing—validating extracted data through domain expert review, ontology grounding to standard databases, and human-in-the-loop corrections.

The presentation highlighted Vision Language Models (VLMs) as complementary tools for extracting data from figures, tables, and spectra, though they are more expensive than text-only approaches. The discussion of agents—LLMs assigned specific roles with access to specialized tools—showed their potential for complex extraction tasks while noting limitations like infinite loops and high token usage. Key challenges identified include domain-specific terminology (especially chemical nomenclature), hallucinations requiring validation, scattered information across papers, lack of standardized benchmarks across fields, scalability costs, and copyright/licensing restrictions that currently favor open-access sources.

The interactive format sparked rich discussion on practical applications: using tools like Edison Scientific for literature searches with 200 free academic credits, the difficulty of comparing data across inconsistent reporting practices in different research groups, the analogy of LLMs as "overly enthusiastic master students" requiring expert oversight, and the value of slow manual reading versus rapid automated extraction for different use cases. Participants discussed potential agent applications for weekly literature monitoring and the broader challenge of keeping up with exponentially growing scientific literature. The group decided to pause meetings for summer and restart in September, with interest expressed in future sessions on agentic workflows and literature tracking strategies.
