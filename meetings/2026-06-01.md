**Summary:** 
This meeting was an open peer discussion on practical tooling and data/code organization for materials research. A recurring theme was the gap between **FAIR goals** and what is manageable in day-to-day experimental work.

Participants compared lightweight workflows built around **VS Code + Git + notebooks**. They also discussed writing and automation patterns, including **LaTeX in VS Code**, **Overleaf** collaboration. Built-in **agentic support** was viewed as useful for code execution, proofreading, and related tasks, and participants noted available educational institutional credits for GitHub Copilot.

For scaling and HPC, the group described an **interactive-to-batch** path: validate a notebook on one dataset, then run many datasets via **Slurm array jobs**, often by injecting parameters into notebook templates and moving heavier logic into `.py` modules.

Participants also noted friction in remote notebook workflows and the common use of **JupyterHub** as a practical interface. No one had direct experience controlling remote nodes through VS Code, though this seemed feasible. Facility data-retention policies (short disk windows, archival storage, restaging on request) were identified as concrete constraints on long-term reproducibility. Institutional choices further limit what can be implemented in practice, and maintaining FAIR data shares was seen as time-consuming with limited short-term incentives.

Overall, the group favored practical, transparent workflows that small teams can maintain, scale when needed, and share with appropriate curation.
