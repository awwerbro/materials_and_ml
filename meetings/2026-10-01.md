**Summary:** Jorden introduced arPLS as an iterative method for estimating and subtracting a smoothly varying baseline from measured signals. It minimizes a weighted least-squares mismatch between the data and a fitted baseline, with a second-difference penalty controlling smoothness. The weights are updated asymmetrically: points above the current baseline are treated as possible signal peaks, while the logistic weighting scheme uses the residual distribution to avoid the persistent positive bias that can arise with simpler asymmetric weighting methods.

Examples covered spectroscopic data (including XRF and EDX) and X-ray diffraction. Applying the correction to each column of time-resolved in-situ XRD data made weak peaks easier to distinguish and supported more reliable tracking of crystallization. The method does not require a predefined background shape, but its result depends on parameter choices: a stronger smoothness constraint can miss changes in the baseline, while a weaker one can follow and remove real peaks. Jorden described fitting a local region around a peak of interest as a practical way to limit this tradeoff.

Discussion emphasized that baseline correction is purpose-dependent: some broad or sloping features may be signal rather than instrumental background, and the method's peak-above-baseline weighting may not directly suit absorption features without adapting the data or method. The group raised questions about noise-distribution assumptions and peak significance; arPLS itself returns a baseline rather than a significance measure, and parameter choices can affect downstream values. Consistent processing and reporting the settings were considered important for comparisons. Participants also suggested exploring automated smoothness selection, for example through cross-validation, while retaining domain judgment to assess whether a fitted baseline is physically plausible.
