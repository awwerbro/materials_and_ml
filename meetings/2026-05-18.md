**Summary:** 
Andreas presented an overview of smoothing experimental data, with a clear distinction between when smoothing is useful (for downstream transforms like differentiation) and when it is risky (direct peak interpretation/fitting, where smoothing can hide or broaden real features). A major discussion point was transparency: smoothing for presentation can be acceptable, but it should always be disclosed.

The strongest focus was on **Total Variation (TV)** approaches and their extensions. The key intuition discussed was that noise appears as high-frequency jagged variation, so adding a regularization term on derivatives can suppress noise while preserving meaningful sharp structure better than simple averaging windows. This was connected to the original TV denoising formulation and to derivative-focused variants.

From there, the group discussed **Whittaker-Eilers smoothing** as a practical, fast implementation of this regularized idea for 1D signals. The appealing part was not only the smoothing quality, but also the more objective selection of the smoothing parameter lambda through cross-validation rather than only visual tuning. In the examples, the method recovered subtle shoulder features in XRD while remaining computationally light.

Other methods were still compared for context: binning, moving-average/Gaussian convolution, and Savitzky-Golay. These were framed as useful baselines, but with caveats such as edge shifts, parameter sensitivity, and the risk of using visually pleasing settings without clear criteria. Practical use cases raised in discussion included SEM image processing and treating time-resolved spectra as 2D data where TV-style methods may be especially effective. The group also emphasized that improving measurement conditions first (when possible) is preferable to relying solely on post-processing.
