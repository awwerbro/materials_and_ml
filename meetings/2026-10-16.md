# October 16, 2026 Meeting

**Date:** 2026-10-16  
**Presenter:** Mariana Fazio  
**Topic:** Deep Learning for Automated XPS Analysis  
**Paper/Resources:**
- [A Shifting Variant Deep Learning Framework for Automated Analysis of XPS Spectra](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.elspec.2026.147624)

**Summary:** Mariana presented a spatial transformer network for classifying functional groups in XPS spectra despite binding-energy shifts, which can arise from sample charging and instrument or sample effects. The model learns to align spectra using relationships between peaks, and in tests with synthetic data it retained higher classification accuracy under shifts than multilayer perceptron and convolutional neural network baselines. Its performance nevertheless varied by functional group and abundance, with false negatives and reduced sensitivity for subtle or low-concentration features. The model was evaluated on synthetic rather than real experimental spectra, leaving validation on diverse measured data as a key next step; the group also distinguished shift-tolerant classification from estimating the shift itself.

Discussion connected the paper's motivation about gaps in XPS expertise to the risk that easier automated interpretation could encourage overconfident conclusions. Participants compared charge-correction practices, including adventitious-carbon referencing and gold nanoparticle references, noting that appropriate choices depend on the sample and measurement context. The group saw potential in the architecture but questioned whether the demonstrated task addresses a sufficiently difficult experimental need, emphasizing that machine learning should be judged against existing practice and validated on real data. Broader discussion considered the value of incremental results and lessons learned, and how AI may accelerate research while increasing the volume of literature.
